Chapter 13: gp2 vs gp3 vs io1 vs io2: Choosing the Right Storage Type

For most AWS RDS workloads running on gp2 today, a single storage type migration to gp3 delivers an immediate 30% cost reduction with zero downtime and no application changes. On a 2TB RDS volume, that is roughly $860 per year saved on storage alone — before accounting for the IOPS and throughput improvements that often eliminate the need for provisioned IOPS entirely. This chapter walks through the mechanics of each storage type, the diagnostic queries that reveal whether your current storage is over- or under-provisioned, the math behind migration decisions, and the automation that makes these changes repeatable across dozens of RDS instances without manual intervention.

---

Understanding the Four Storage Types: Pricing and Architecture

AWS RDS offers four EBS-backed storage types. Each is optimized for a different cost-performance trade-off, and the pricing differences between them are large enough to matter at scale.

gp2 (General Purpose SSD, Generation 2)

gp2 is the original general-purpose option. Pricing sits at approximately $0.115 per GB-month in us-east-1. The key architectural constraint is that IOPS are tied directly to volume size: you receive 3 IOPS per GB, with a baseline of 100 IOPS and a hard ceiling of 16,000 IOPS. A 500GB gp2 volume delivers 1,500 IOPS. To reach 16,000 IOPS on gp2, you must provision at least 5,334GB of storage — even if your database is only 400GB in size. Teams frequently over-provision gp2 storage purely for IOPS, paying for unused space.

gp2 also uses a burst credit system for volumes under 1TB. Volumes smaller than 333GB can burst to 3,000 IOPS when credits are available, but sustained workloads exhaust those credits and drop back to baseline. This creates invisible performance cliffs that only appear under load.

gp3 (General Purpose SSD, Generation 3)

gp3 decouples IOPS from storage size. Base price is $0.08 per GB-month — a 30% reduction versus gp2. Every gp3 volume includes 3,000 IOPS and 125 MB/s throughput at no additional charge. IOPS can be independently scaled up to 16,000 for $0.005 per provisioned IOPS-month above the 3,000 baseline, and throughput up to 1,000 MB/s for $0.04 per MB/s-month above 125 MB/s.

For a 1TB volume:

  • → gp2: 1,024GB × $0.115 = **$117.76/month**, delivers 3,072 IOPS
  • → gp3: 1,024GB × $0.08 = **$81.92/month**, delivers 3,000 IOPS (essentially the same), saves **$35.84/month** or **$430/year**

    • For a 2TB volume with no extra IOPS needed:

  • → gp2: 2,048GB × $0.115 = **$235.52/month**
  • → gp3: 2,048GB × $0.08 = **$163.84/month**, saves **$71.68/month** or **$860/year**

    • io1 (Provisioned IOPS SSD, Generation 1)

    io1 is designed for I/O-intensive workloads requiring sustained, consistent IOPS. Storage costs $0.125 per GB-month, and IOPS are independently provisioned at $0.065 per IOPS-month. The IOPS-to-GB ratio is capped at 50:1. A 500GB io1 volume can provision a maximum of 25,000 IOPS. Maximum IOPS is 64,000 on Nitro-based instances.

    The cost model makes io1 expensive at high IOPS. A volume provisioned at 10,000 IOPS on 200GB of storage costs:

  • → Storage: 200 × $0.125 = $25.00
  • → IOPS: 10,000 × $0.065 = $650.00
  • → **Total: $675.00/month**

    • io2 (Provisioned IOPS SSD, Generation 2)

    io2 replaces io1 for most use cases. It offers higher durability (99.999% versus 99.8% for io1), a 500:1 IOPS-to-GB ratio, and tiered pricing that makes high IOPS cheaper than io1 at volume. As of 2024 pricing in us-east-1:

  • → Storage: **$0.125 per GB-month** (same as io1)
  • → IOPS up to 32,000: **$0.065 per IOPS-month**
  • → IOPS from 32,001–64,000: **$0.046 per IOPS-month**
  • → IOPS above 64,000: **$0.032 per IOPS-month**

    • io2 Block Express extends the ceiling to 256,000 IOPS and 4,000 MB/s throughput, available on r6i, m6i, and similar Nitro instances. For any workload currently on io1, migrating to io2 at the same IOPS level costs the same or less and delivers meaningfully higher durability. There is no reason to choose io1 for new deployments.

    Diagnosing Your Current Storage: Are You Over-Provisioned?

    Before making any changes, collect four weeks of actual storage metrics. The goal is to determine three things: actual IOPS consumed, actual throughput consumed, and the percentage of time the storage is at or near its provisioned limit.

    Querying CloudWatch for RDS Storage Metrics

    # Pull 30-day average and peak IOPS for an RDS instance
# Replace DB_INSTANCE_IDENTIFIER with your actual instance ID

DB_ID="prod-sqlserver-01"
START_TIME=$(date -u -d '30 days ago' +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)
END_TIME=$(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)

echo "=== Read IOPS (Average and Maximum) ==="
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
  --namespace AWS/RDS \
  --metric-name ReadIOPS \
  --dimensions Name=DBInstanceIdentifier,Value=$DB_ID \
  --start-time $START_TIME \
  --end-time $END_TIME \
  --period 3600 \
  --statistics Average Maximum \
  --output table

echo "=== Write IOPS (Average and Maximum) ==="
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
  --namespace AWS/RDS \
  --metric-name WriteIOPS \
  --dimensions Name=DBInstanceIdentifier,Value=$DB_ID \
  --start-time $START_TIME \
  --end-time $END_TIME \
  --period 3600 \
  --statistics Average Maximum \
  --output table

echo "=== Read Throughput MB/s ==="
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
  --namespace AWS/RDS \

  --end-time $END_TIME \

echo "=== Storage Queue Depth (key saturation signal) ==="
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \

echo "=== Burst Balance (gp2 only — 0% means sustained IOPS pressure) ==="
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \

  --metric-name BurstBalance \

  --start-time $START_TIME \
  --end-time $END_TIME \
  --period 3600 \
  --statistics Average Minimum \
  --output table

    Interpreting the Results

    Three patterns emerge from this data:

    1. Peak IOPS stays below 2,500 on a gp2 volume of any size — this is a candidate for gp3 with no additional IOPS provisioning. The included 3,000 IOPS on gp3 covers the workload entirely.

    2. BurstBalance frequently drops below 20% — the instance is hitting the burst ceiling. If the volume is under 1TB, either the workload genuinely needs more IOPS (evaluate io2), or the volume should be sized to the performance tier it actually needs on gp3 without the credit system.

    3. DiskQueueDepth regularly exceeds 1.0 — storage is a bottleneck. This is not a candidate for cost reduction yet; it needs either more IOPS or a storage type upgrade evaluated for performance, not cost.

    Querying SQL Server for I/O Patterns at the File Level

    -- SQL Server: find database files with the highest physical I/O pressure
-- Run against the target database on RDS SQL Server
-- sys.dm_io_virtual_file_stats accumulates since the last SQL Server restart

SELECT
    DB_NAME(vfs.database_id)                    AS database_name,
    mf.name                                     AS logical_file_name,
    mf.physical_name,
    vfs.io_stall_read_ms,
    vfs.io_stall_write_ms,
    vfs.io_stall_read_ms + vfs.io_stall_write_ms AS io_stall_total_ms,
    vfs.num_of_reads,
    vfs.num_of_writes,
    vfs.num_of_bytes_read    / 1048576.0        AS mb_read,
    vfs.num_of_bytes_written / 1048576.0        AS mb_written,
    -- Average read latency in ms (high values indicate storage pressure)
    CASE WHEN vfs.num_of_reads = 0 THEN 0
         ELSE vfs.io_stall_read_ms * 1.0 / vfs.num_of_reads
    END                                         AS avg_read_latency_ms,
    CASE WHEN vfs.num_of_writes = 0 THEN 0
         ELSE vfs.io_stall_write_ms * 1.0 / vfs.num_of_writes
    END                                         AS avg_write_latency_ms
FROM
    sys.dm_io_virtual_file_stats(NULL, NULL) AS vfs
    JOIN sys.master_files AS mf
        ON vfs.database_id = mf.database_id
        AND vfs.file_id    = mf.file_id
ORDER BY
    io_stall_total_ms DESC;

-- SQL Server: identify the most I/O-intensive queries currently in the plan cache
SELECT TOP 20
    qs.total_logical_reads                          AS total_logical_reads,
    qs.total_physical_reads                         AS total_physical_reads,
    qs.total_logical_writes                         AS total_logical_writes,
    qs.execution_count,
    qs.total_physical_reads / NULLIF(qs.execution_count, 0)
                                                    AS avg_physical_reads,
    SUBSTRING(qt.text, (qs.statement_start_offset/2) + 1,
        ((CASE qs.statement_end_offset
            WHEN -1 THEN DATALENGTH(qt.text)
            ELSE qs.statement_end_offset
          END - qs.statement_start_offset) / 2) + 1)
                                                    AS statement_text,
    qp.query_plan
FROM
    sys.dm_exec_query_stats      AS qs
    CROSS APPLY sys.dm_exec_sql_text(qs.sql_handle)   AS qt
    CROSS APPLY sys.dm_exec_query_plan(qs.plan_handle) AS qp
WHERE
    qs.total_physical_reads > 5000
ORDER BY
    qs.total_physical_reads DESC;

    A cache hit ratio consistently above 99% across most tables signals that your workload is largely memory-bound — physical IOPS are a secondary concern, and gp3's baseline allocation is almost certainly sufficient.

    Running the Migration Cost Math

    The decision framework for storage type selection follows a simple flowprint based on actual IOPS requirements:

    Start from measured peak IOPS, not from the tier name. Under 3K, gp3's baseline is the cheapest correct answer; 3K–16K adds provisioned IOPS on gp3; 16K–64K moves to io2; beyond 64K needs io2 Block Express on a Nitro instance. The arrows run left to right in cost order — each step up is only justified when your measurements put you in that band.

    Peak IOPS needed?
├── < 3,000:         gp3 (no extra IOPS, lowest cost)
├── 3,000 – 16,000:  gp3 with additional provisioned IOPS
├── 16,000 – 64,000: io2
└── > 64,000:        io2 Block Express (requires Nitro instance class)

    The crossover point where io2 becomes cheaper than gp3 with additional IOPS sits around 16,000 IOPS for volumes under 320GB. For larger volumes, the per-GB price difference adds up in gp3's favor until IOPS demands are extreme.

    Python Script: Storage Cost Calculator and Migration Recommendation

    #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
rds_storage_advisor.py
Calculates current vs. optimized RDS storage costs and emits migration recommendations.
Pulls live data from CloudWatch and RDS API, then computes cost differentials.

Usage:
    python3 rds_storage_advisor.py --region us-east-1 --profile prod
    python3 rds_storage_advisor.py --instance-id prod-sqlserver-01
"""

import argparse
import json
from datetime import datetime, timedelta, timezone

import boto3

# ── Pricing (us-east-1, on-demand, as of Q1 2025) ──────────────────────────
# Update these when AWS adjusts pricing
PRICING = {
    "gp2": {
        "storage_per_gb": 0.115,
        "iops_per_iops": 0.0,       # IOPS tied to storage
        "throughput_per_mbs": 0.0,
    },
    "gp3": {
        "storage_per_gb": 0.080,
        "base_iops": 3000,
        "base_throughput_mbs": 125,
        "iops_per_iops": 0.005,     # per IOPS above 3,000
        "throughput_per_mbs": 0.04, # per MB/s above 125
    },
    "io1": {
        "storage_per_gb": 0.125,
        "iops_per_iops": 0.065,
    },
    "io2": {
        "storage_per_gb": 0.125,
        "iops_tiers": [
            (32000, 0.065),
            (64000, 0.046),
            (float("inf"), 0.032),
        ],
    },
}


def get_rds_instances(session, instance_id=None):
    rds = session.client("rds")
    if instance_id:
        resp = rds.describe_db_instances(DBInstanceIdentifier=instance_id)
    else:
        resp = rds.describe_db_instances()
    return resp["DBInstances"]


def get_cloudwatch_stat(cw, db_id, metric, stat, days=30):
    """Return the maximum value of a CloudWatch metric over the past N days."""
    resp = cw.get_metric_statistics(
        Namespace="AWS/RDS",
        MetricName=metric,
        Dimensions=[{"Name": "DBInstanceIdentifier", "Value": db_id}],
        StartTime=datetime.now(timezone.utc) - timedelta(days=days),
        EndTime=datetime.now(timezone.utc),
        Period=3600,
        Statistics=[stat],
    )
    points = resp.get("Datapoints", [])
    if not points:
        return 0.0
    return max(p[stat] for p in points)


def compute_gp2_cost(size_gb):
    return size_gb * PRICING["gp2"]["storage_per_gb"]


def compute_gp3_cost(size_gb, required_iops, required_throughput_mbs):
    p = PRICING["gp3"]
    storage_cost = size_gb * p["storage_per_gb"]
    extra_iops = max(0, required_iops - p["base_iops"])
    extra_throughput = max(0, required_throughput_mbs - p["base_throughput_mbs"])
    iops_cost = extra_iops * p["iops_per_iops"]
    throughput_cost = extra_throughput * p["throughput_per_mbs"]
    return storage_cost + iops_cost + throughput_cost, {
        "storage": round(storage_cost, 2),
        "iops": round(iops_cost, 2),
        "throughput": round(throughput_cost, 2),
    }


def compute_io2_cost(size_gb, required_iops):
    storage_cost = size_gb * PRICING["io2"]["storage_per_gb"]
    iops_cost = 0.0
    remaining = required_iops
    prev_ceiling = 0
    for ceiling, rate in PRICING["io2"]["iops_tiers"]:
        tier_iops = min(remaining, ceiling - prev_ceiling)
        if tier_iops <= 0:
            break
        iops_cost += tier_iops * rate
        remaining -= tier_iops
        prev_ceiling = ceiling
    return storage_cost + iops_cost


def make_recommendation(instance, peak_iops, peak_throughput_mbs):
    storage_type = instance.get("StorageType", "gp2")
    size_gb = instance["AllocatedStorage"]
    iops_provisioned = instance.get("Iops", 0) or 0
    db_id = instance["DBInstanceIdentifier"]

    current_cost = 0.0
    if storage_type == "gp2":
        current_cost = compute_gp2_cost(size_gb)
        effective_iops = max(100, size_gb * 3)
    elif storage_type == "gp3":
        current_cost, _ = compute_gp3_cost(size_gb, iops_provisioned, peak_throughput_mbs)
        effective_iops = iops_provisioned
    elif storage_type in ("io1", "io2"):
        current_cost = (size_gb * PRICING[storage_type]["storage_per_gb"] +
                        iops_provisioned * PRICING.get("io1", PRICING["io2"])
                        .get("iops_per_iops", 0.065))
        effective_iops = iops_provisioned

    # Add 20% headroom to peak IOPS for recommendation
    target_iops = max(3000, int(peak_iops * 1.20))
    target_throughput = max(125, int(peak_throughput_mbs * 1.20))

    gp3_cost, gp3_breakdown = compute_gp3_cost(size_gb, target_iops, target_throughput)
    io2_cost = compute_io2_cost(size_gb, target_iops)

    if target_iops <= 16000:
        recommended_type = "gp3"
        recommended_cost = gp3_cost
    else:
        recommended_type = "io2"
        recommended_cost = io2_cost

    monthly_savings = current_cost - recommended_cost
    annual_savings = monthly_savings * 12

    return {
        "instance_id": db_id,
        "current_type": storage_type,
        "size_gb": size_gb,
        "current_provisioned_iops": iops_provisioned,
        "peak_actual_iops_30d": round(peak_iops, 0),
        "peak_actual_throughput_mbs": round(peak_throughput_mbs, 2),
        "target_iops_with_headroom": target_iops,
        "current_monthly_cost_usd": round(current_cost, 2),
        "recommended_type": recommended_type,
        "recommended_monthly_cost_usd": round(recommended_cost, 2),
        "monthly_savings_usd": round(monthly_savings, 2),
        "annual_savings_usd": round(annual_savings, 2),
        "gp3_breakdown": gp3_breakdown,
        "action": (
            f"aws rds modify-db-instance --db-instance-identifier {db_id} "
            f"--storage-type {recommended_type} "
            f"--iops {target_iops} "
            f"--apply-immediately"
            if recommended_type in ("io2",)
            else
            f"aws rds modify-db-instance --db-instance-identifier {db_id} "
            f"--storage-type gp3 "
            f"--iops {target_iops} "
            f"--storage-throughput {target_throughput} "
            f"--apply-immediately"
        ),
    }


def main():
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="RDS Storage Type Advisor")
    parser.add_argument("--region", default="us-east-1")
    parser.add_argument("--profile", default=None)
    parser.add_argument("--instance-id", default=None)
    parser.add_argument("--output", choices=["json", "text"], default="text")
    args = parser.parse_args()

    session = boto3.Session(region_name=args.region, profile_name=args.profile)
    cw = session.client("cloudwatch")

    instances = get_rds_instances(session, args.instance_id)
    recommendations = []
    total_current = 0.0
    total_recommended = 0.0

    for inst in instances:
        db_id = inst["DBInstanceIdentifier"]
        print(f"Analyzing {db_id}...")

        peak_read_iops = get_cloudwatch_stat(cw, db_id, "ReadIOPS", "Maximum")
        peak_write_iops = get_cloudwatch_stat(cw, db_id, "WriteIOPS", "Maximum")
        peak_iops = peak_read_iops + peak_write_iops

        peak_read_tp = get_cloudwatch_stat(cw, db_id, "ReadThroughput", "Maximum")
        peak_write_tp = get_cloudwatch_stat(cw, db_id, "WriteThroughput", "Maximum")
        peak_throughput_mbs = (peak_read_tp + peak_write_tp) / (1024 * 1024)

        rec = make_recommendation(inst, peak_iops, peak_throughput_mbs)
        recommendations.append(rec)
        total_current += rec["current_monthly_cost_usd"]
        total_recommended += rec["recommended_monthly_cost_usd"]

    summary = {
        "total_instances_analyzed": len(recommendations),
        "total_current_monthly_storage_cost_usd": round(total_current, 2),
        "total_recommended_monthly_cost_usd": round(total_recommended, 2),
        "total_monthly_savings_usd": round(total_current - total_recommended, 2),
        "total_annual_savings_usd": round((total_current - total_recommended) * 12, 2),
        "recommendations": recommendations,
    }

    if args.output == "json":
        print(json.dumps(summary, indent=2))
    else:
        print("\n" + "=" * 70)
        print(f"  RDS Storage Advisor Summary — {args.region}")
        print("=" * 70)
        print(f"  Instances analyzed:        {summary['total_instances_analyzed']}")
        print(f"  Current monthly cost:      ${summary['total_current_monthly_storage_cost_usd']:>10,.2f}")
        print(f"  Optimized monthly cost:    ${summary['total_recommended_monthly_cost_usd']:>10,.2f}")
        print(f"  Monthly savings:           ${summary['total_monthly_savings_usd']:>10,.2f}")
        print(f"  Annual savings:            ${summary['total_annual_savings_usd']:>10,.2f}")
        print("=" * 70)
        print()
        for rec in recommendations:
            change = "✓ KEEP" if rec["monthly_savings_usd"] <= 0 else "→ MIGRATE"
            print(f"  [{change}] {rec['instance_id']}")
            print(f"          Current: {rec['current_type']} / {rec['size_gb']}GB / "
                  f"{rec['current_provisioned_iops']} IOPS → ${rec['current_monthly_cost_usd']:.2f}/mo")
            print(f"          Recommended: {rec['recommended_type']} / "
                  f"{rec['target_iops_with_headroom']} IOPS → ${rec['recommended_monthly_cost_usd']:.2f}/mo")
            print(f"          Savings: ${rec['monthly_savings_usd']:.2f}/mo (${rec['annual_savings_usd']:.2f}/yr)")
            if rec["monthly_savings_usd"] > 0:
                print(f"          Command: {rec['action']}")
            print()


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

    Running this script across a fleet of twenty RDS instances is a common first step in a storage optimization engagement. The output immediately surfaces which instances are on gp2 where the workload fits within gp3's baseline, which are over-provisioned on io1 or io2, and which have legitimate IOPS demands that justify their current configuration.

    Executing the Migration: gp2 to gp3 Step by Step

    The actual migration is an online operation managed by AWS. RDS modifies the storage type in the background with no downtime, though the modification may take from a few minutes to several hours depending on volume size. Applications remain connected throughout.

    Pre-Migration Checklist

    Before submitting the modify command, verify three things:

    1. Maintenance window and Multi-AZ status — for Multi-AZ deployments, the modification applies first to the standby, then fails over, then applies to the former primary. This produces a brief failover event (typically 20–30 seconds). Schedule the modification during a low-traffic period if your application is sensitive to failovers.

    2. Storage autoscaling — if RDS storage autoscaling is enabled, verify the maximum autoscale threshold won't trigger immediately after the migration in a way that inflates gp3 costs unexpectedly.

    3. I/O-related settings — if you tuned any I/O-related SQL Server settings (for example recovery interval or checkpoint behavior) based on io1 performance characteristics, revisit those after migration to gp3.

    The Migration Command

    # Step 1: Capture current configuration before any change
aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --query 'DBInstances[0].{Type:StorageType,GB:AllocatedStorage,IOPS:Iops,Engine:Engine}' \
  --output table

# Step 2: Submit the storage type modification
# --apply-immediately causes the modification to start now rather than
# waiting for the next maintenance window. Use this during off-peak hours.
aws rds modify-db-instance \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --storage-type gp3 \
  --iops 3000 \
  --storage-throughput 125 \
  --apply-immediately

# If your workload needs more than 3,000 IOPS (from CloudWatch analysis):
aws rds modify-db-instance \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --storage-type gp3 \
  --iops 6000 \
  --storage-throughput 250 \
  --apply-immediately

# Step 3: Monitor the modification status
# Status will transition: available -> modifying -> available
aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --query 'DBInstances[0].{Status:DBInstanceStatus,PendingModifications:PendingModifiedValues}' \
  --output table

# Poll until modification completes (typically 15–60 minutes for volumes under 500GB)
watch -n 60 "aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --query 'DBInstances[0].DBInstanceStatus' \
  --output text"

    Post-Migration Validation

    # Confirm the new storage type is in effect
aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --db-instance-identifier prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --query 'DBInstances[0].{Type:StorageType,IOPS:Iops,Throughput:StorageThroughput,GB:AllocatedStorage}' \
  --output table

# Verify DiskQueueDepth hasn't increased post-migration (watch for 24 hours)
aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
  --namespace AWS/RDS \
  --metric-name DiskQueueDepth \
  --dimensions Name=DBInstanceIdentifier,Value=prod-sqlserver-01 \
  --start-time $(date -u -d '4 hours ago' +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ) \
  --end-time $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ) \
  --period 300 \
  --statistics Average Maximum \
  --output table

# Verify ReadLatency and WriteLatency are stable post-migration
for METRIC in ReadLatency WriteLatency; do
  echo "=== $METRIC ==="
  aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
    --namespace AWS/RDS \
    --metric-name $METRIC \
    --dimensions Name=DBInstanceIdentifier,Value=prod-sqlserver-01 \
    --start-time $(date -u -d '4 hours ago' +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ) \
    --end-time $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ) \
    --period 300 \
    --statistics Average Maximum \
    --output table
done

    If DiskQueueDepth climbs above 1.0 or latency metrics increase materially after migration, the workload needs more IOPS than the baseline 3,000. Increase provisioned IOPS on the gp3 volume with another modify-db-instance call — this adjustment takes effect within minutes and does not require a storage type change.

    SQL Server Equivalent: Diagnosing I/O on RDS SQL Server

    Reading the Results

  • → `avg_read_latency_ms` above 20ms on data files indicates the storage tier is under pressure. Below 5ms is healthy for gp3; below 1ms is expected for io2 at moderate IOPS.
  • → `total_physical_reads` in the query cache identifies statements doing large sequential scans or missing index seeks — these drive IOPS requirements up and are a better target for query or index optimization than storage upgrades.

    • The same CloudWatch-based IOPS analysis applies identically to SQL Server RDS. The ReadIOPS, WriteIOPS, and DiskQueueDepth metrics are engine-agnostic.

    When gp3 Is Not the Right Answer: io2 Decision Criteria

    gp3 tops out at 16,000 IOPS. For workloads that genuinely need more, io2 is the path forward. The four scenarios where io2 is warranted are:

    1. OLTP with sustained high-concurrency writes

    Transaction processing systems with hundreds of concurrent sessions issuing small writes — payment processing, order management, event ingestion — can saturate gp3's ceiling even on well-tuned instances. If CloudWatch shows peak combined IOPS consistently above 12,000 (leaving no headroom before 16,000), io2 provides the additional ceiling.

    2. Large databases with full-scan analytical queries on the OLTP instance

    A 4TB OLTP database running nightly batch reports that scan large portions of the dataset will generate sequential read IOPS spikes that gp3 cannot always absorb. The correct architectural answer is read replicas or a separate analytical environment — but while that refactoring is pending, io2 provides headroom.

    3. Oracle workloads with REDO log contention

    Oracle on RDS frequently generates write IOPS patterns concentrated on redo log files. These are short, synchronous writes that are highly latency-sensitive. io2's consistent single-digit millisecond latency under load matters more for Oracle redo than raw throughput.

    4. Instances where the 50:1 gp3 IOPS-to-GB ratio creates oversized volumes

    gp3 limits IOPS to 500 per GB (up to the 16,000 ceiling). A 100GB database needing 10,000 IOPS on gp3 requires provisioning 200GB of storage to reach that IOPS level. io2's 500:1 ratio means the same 100GB database can be provisioned with 100GB of storage at 10,000 IOPS. In this narrow case, io2 is both cheaper and better-fit.

    Automating Storage Type Migrations Across a Fleet

    Running the advisory script and executing individual CLI commands works for a handful of instances. At scale — dozens or hundreds of RDS instances across multiple accounts — the process needs to be automated, gated by approval, and logged.

    The following Terraform configuration combined with a Python Lambda function provides a repeatable, auditable migration pipeline.

    Terraform: RDS Instance with Explicit Storage Type and Tagging

    # rds_storage_optimized.tf
# Enforces gp3 as the default storage type for all new RDS instances.
# Existing instances are migrated via the Lambda automation below.

variable "db_instance_map" {
  description = "Map of RDS instance configurations"
  type = map(object({
    engine          = string
    engine_version  = string
    instance_class  = string
    allocated_gb    = number
    iops            = number
    throughput_mbs  = number
    multi_az        = bool
    environment     = string
  }))
}

resource "aws_db_instance" "optimized" {
  for_each = var.db_instance_map

  identifier        = each.key
  engine            = each.value.engine
  engine_version    = each.value.engine_version
  instance_class    = each.value.instance_class
  allocated_storage = each.value.allocated_gb

  # Always use gp3 unless IOPS demand exceeds 16,000
  storage_type      = each.value.iops > 16000 ? "io2" : "gp3"
  iops              = each.value.iops > 3000 ? each.value.iops : null
  storage_throughput = each.value.throughput_mbs > 125 ? each.value.throughput_mbs : null

  multi_az               = each.value.multi_az
  storage_encrypted      = true

  tags = {
    StorageOptimized  = "true"
    StorageReviewedAt = timestamp()
    Environment       = each.value.environment
    ManagedBy         = "terraform"
  }

  lifecycle {
    # Prevent accidental storage type regressions
    precondition {
      condition     = each.value.iops <= 16000 || contains(["io2"], self.storage_type)
      error_message = "Instances requiring >16,000 IOPS must use io2 storage type."
    }
  }
}

    Python Lambda: Scheduled Storage Optimization Sweep

    #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
lambda_storage_optimizer.py
Scheduled Lambda (runs weekly) that identifies gp2 RDS instances,
computes recommended gp3 configurations, and either applies changes
automatically (for non-production) or creates a JIRA/SNS ticket for review.

Environment variables:
    AUTO_APPLY_ENVS   - comma-separated list of environments where changes
                        are applied automatically (e.g., "dev,staging")
    APPROVAL_SNS_ARN  - SNS topic for production change approvals
    DRY_RUN           - set to "true" to log recommendations without acting
"""

import json
import logging
import os
from datetime import datetime, timedelta, timezone

import boto3

logger = logging.getLogger()
logger.setLevel(logging.INFO)

AUTO_APPLY_ENVS = set(os.environ.get("AUTO_APPLY_ENVS", "dev,staging").split(","))
APPROVAL_SNS_ARN = os.environ.get("APPROVAL_SNS_ARN", "")
DRY_RUN = os.environ.get("DRY_RUN", "false").lower() == "true"

# Minimum monthly savings threshold to trigger a recommendation ($)
MIN_SAVINGS_THRESHOLD = 10.0


def get_instance_environment(instance):
    """Extract environment tag from RDS instance tags."""
    tags = {t["Key"]: t["Value"] for t in instance.get("TagList", [])}
    return tags.get("Environment", tags.get("environment", "unknown")).lower()


def should_auto_apply(environment):
    return environment in AUTO_APPLY_ENVS and not DRY_RUN


def compute_gp3_recommendation(size_gb, peak_iops, peak_throughput_mbs):
    """Return recommended gp3 IOPS and throughput with 20% headroom."""
    target_iops = max(3000, int(peak_iops * 1.20))
    target_throughput = max(125, int(peak_throughput_mbs * 1.20))
    target_iops = min(target_iops, 16000)    # gp3 ceiling
    target_throughput = min(target_throughput, 1000)  # gp3 ceiling
    return target_iops, target_throughput


def get_peak_metric(cw, db_id, metric_name, days=30):
    resp = cw.get_metric_statistics(
        Namespace="AWS/RDS",
        MetricName=metric_name,
        Dimensions=[{"Name": "DBInstanceIdentifier", "Value": db_id}],
        StartTime=datetime.now(timezone.utc) - timedelta(days=days),
        EndTime=datetime.now(timezone.utc),
        Period=3600,
        Statistics=["Maximum"],
    )
    points = resp.get("Datapoints", [])
    return max((p["Maximum"] for p in points), default=0.0)


def apply_gp3_migration(rds_client, db_id, target_iops, target_throughput):
    logger.info(
        "Applying gp3 migration: %s → iops=%d throughput=%d",
        db_id, target_iops, target_throughput
    )
    rds_client.modify_db_instance(
        DBInstanceIdentifier=db_id,
        StorageType="gp3",
        Iops=target_iops,
        StorageThroughput=target_throughput,
        ApplyImmediately=True,
    )


def send_approval_request(sns_client, recommendation):
    message = {
        "subject": f"RDS Storage Migration Pending Approval: {recommendation['instance_id']}",
        "body": recommendation,
        "requested_at": datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat(),
    }
    sns_client.publish(
        TopicArn=APPROVAL_SNS_ARN,
        Subject=message["subject"],
        Message=json.dumps(message, indent=2),
    )
    logger.info("Approval request sent for %s", recommendation["instance_id"])


def lambda_handler(event, context):
    region = os.environ.get("AWS_REGION", "us-east-1")
    session = boto3.Session(region_name=region)
    rds = session.client("rds")
    cw = session.client("cloudwatch")
    sns = session.client("sns") if APPROVAL_SNS_ARN else None

    paginator = rds.get_paginator("describe_db_instances")
    results = {"applied": [], "pending_approval": [], "skipped": [], "errors": []}

    for page in paginator.paginate():
        for inst in page["DBInstances"]:
            db_id = inst["DBInstanceIdentifier"]
            storage_type = inst.get("StorageType", "unknown")

            # Only process gp2 instances — gp3/io2 handled separately
            if storage_type != "gp2":
                results["skipped"].append(
                    {"instance_id": db_id, "reason": f"already on {storage_type}"}
                )
                continue

            # Skip instances currently being modified
            if inst.get("DBInstanceStatus") != "available":
                results["skipped"].append(
                    {"instance_id": db_id, "reason": f"status={inst.get('DBInstanceStatus')}"}
                )
                continue

            try:
                size_gb = inst["AllocatedStorage"]
                environment = get_instance_environment(inst)

                peak_read_iops = get_peak_metric(cw, db_id, "ReadIOPS")
                peak_write_iops = get_peak_metric(cw, db_id, "WriteIOPS")
                peak_iops = peak_read_iops + peak_write_iops

                peak_read_tp = get_peak_metric(cw, db_id, "ReadThroughput")
                peak_write_tp = get_peak_metric(cw, db_id, "WriteThroughput")
                peak_throughput_mbs = (peak_read_tp + peak_write_tp) / (1024 * 1024)

                target_iops, target_throughput = compute_gp3_recommendation(
                    size_gb, peak_iops, peak_throughput_mbs
                )

                # Cost delta
                current_cost = size_gb * 0.115
                gp3_storage = size_gb * 0.080
                gp3_iops_cost = max(0, target_iops - 3000) * 0.005
                gp3_tp_cost = max(0, target_throughput - 125) * 0.04
                recommended_cost = gp3_storage + gp3_iops_cost + gp3_tp_cost
                monthly_savings = current_cost - recommended_cost

                if monthly_savings < MIN_SAVINGS_THRESHOLD:
                    results["skipped"].append(
                        {"instance_id": db_id,
                         "reason": f"savings ${monthly_savings:.2f}/mo below threshold"}
                    )
                    continue

                recommendation = {
                    "instance_id": db_id,
                    "environment": environment,
                    "size_gb": size_gb,
                    "current_type": "gp2",
                    "peak_iops_30d": round(peak_iops, 0),
                    "target_iops": target_iops,
                    "target_throughput_mbs": target_throughput,
                    "current_monthly_cost": round(current_cost, 2),
                    "recommended_monthly_cost": round(recommended_cost, 2),
                    "monthly_savings": round(monthly_savings, 2),
                    "annual_savings": round(monthly_savings * 12, 2),
                }

                if should_auto_apply(environment):
                    apply_gp3_migration(rds, db_id, target_iops, target_throughput)
                    results["applied"].append(recommendation)
                elif sns and APPROVAL_SNS_ARN:
                    send_approval_request(sns, recommendation)
                    results["pending_approval"].append(recommendation)
                else:
                    logger.info("DRY RUN — would migrate: %s", json.dumps(recommendation))
                    results["pending_approval"].append(recommendation)

            except Exception as exc:
                logger.error("Error processing %s: %s", db_id, str(exc))
                results["errors"].append({"instance_id": db_id, "error": str(exc)})

    total_applied_savings = sum(r["annual_savings"] for r in results["applied"])
    total_pending_savings = sum(r["annual_savings"] for r in results["pending_approval"])

    logger.info(
        "Sweep complete. Applied: %d ($%.2f/yr saved). Pending approval: %d ($%.2f/yr potential). "
        "Skipped: %d. Errors: %d.",
        len(results["applied"]), total_applied_savings,
        len(results["pending_approval"]), total_pending_savings,
        len(results["skipped"]), len(results["errors"]),
    )

    return {
        "statusCode": 200,
        "body": json.dumps(results),
    }

    Deploy this Lambda on a weekly EventBridge schedule. For dev and staging environments, migrations apply automatically. For production, the SNS notification delivers a structured JSON summary to your operations team, including the exact CLI command needed to execute the change after human review.

    Monitoring Storage Costs Over Time with Cost Explorer Tags

    After completing migrations, the final step is establishing ongoing visibility so storage type regressions — new gp2 instances deployed via manual console clicks or unreviewed Terraform — are caught quickly.

    #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
storage_cost_tracker.py
Queries AWS Cost Explorer for RDS storage costs broken down by storage type.
Run monthly to track optimization progress and detect regressions.
"""

import boto3
from datetime import datetime, date
from dateutil.relativedelta import relativedelta


def get_rds_storage_costs_by_type(months_back=3):
    ce = boto3.client("ce", region_name="us-east-1")

    end_date = date.today().replace(day=1)
    start_date = end_date - relativedelta(months=months_back)

    response = ce.get_cost_and_usage(
        TimePeriod={
            "Start": start_date.strftime("%Y-%m-%d"),
            "End": end_date.strftime("%Y-%m-%d"),
        },
        Granularity="MONTHLY",
        Filter={
            "And": [
                {
                    "Dimensions": {
                        "Key": "SERVICE",
                        "Values": ["Amazon Relational Database Service"],
                    }
                },
                {
                    "Dimensions": {
                        "Key": "USAGE_TYPE_GROUP",
                        "Values": [
                            "RDS: Storage",
                            "RDS: Provisioned IOPS",
                        ],
                    }
                },
            ]
        },
        GroupBy=[
            {"Type": "DIMENSION", "Key": "USAGE_TYPE"},
        ],
        Metrics=["UnblendedCost"],
    )

    print(f"\nRDS Storage Cost Breakdown ({start_date} to {end_date})")
    print("=" * 65)

    for period in response["ResultsByTime"]:
        period_start = period["TimePeriod"]["Start"]
        print(f"\n  {period_start[:7]}")
        print(f"  {'Usage Type':<50} {'Cost':>10}")
        print(f"  {'-'*50} {'-'*10}")

        total = 0.0
        for group in sorted(period["Groups"], key=lambda g: g["Keys"][0]):
            usage_type = group["Keys"][0]
            cost = float(group["Metrics"]["UnblendedCost"]["Amount"])
            total += cost
            # Highlight gp2 costs as candidates for optimization
            marker = " ← gp2 (optimize)" if "gp2" in usage_type.lower() else ""
            print(f"  {usage_type:<50} ${cost:>9.2f}{marker}")

        print(f"  {'TOTAL':<50} ${total:>9.2f}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    get_rds_storage_costs_by_type(months_back=6)

    Set up a monthly cost anomaly detector in AWS Cost Explorer to alert when RDS storage costs increase month-over-month by more than a defined threshold. The combination of the anomaly detector and this tracking script creates a closed loop: migrations reduce cost, the tracker confirms the reduction, and anomaly detection flags any regression caused by new deployments.

    Key Takeaways

  • → **gp3 is the default choice for virtually all RDS workloads under 16,000 IOPS.** Its 30% lower per-GB cost versus gp2, combined with decoupled IOPS and throughput, eliminates the most common reason teams over-provision gp2 storage — chasing IOPS through volume size.
  • → **Measure before migrating.** Four weeks of CloudWatch data on `ReadIOPS`, `WriteIOPS`, `DiskQueueDepth`, and `BurstBalance` determines whether the included 3,000 IOPS on gp3 covers the workload or whether additional IOPS provisioning is needed. Migrating a burst-exhausted gp2 instance to gp3 at 3,000 IOPS without this check can introduce a latency regression that is invisible until production load hits.
  • → **io2 is warranted only above 16,000 IOPS, for Oracle redo log workloads, or when latency consistency at the storage layer is a hard requirement.** For everything else, the gp3 cost advantage is significant enough that io2 is difficult to justify on economic grounds alone.
  • → **The migration itself is online and low-risk.** RDS handles the storage type change in the background with no application downtime. The only operational consideration is the brief Multi-AZ failover event that occurs mid-migration on high-availability instances, which can be scheduled outside peak traffic windows.
  • → **Automation prevents regression.** A weekly Lambda sweep that identifies new gp2 instances and either auto-migrates them in non-production environments or routes them to an approval workflow ensures that storage cost savings compound over time rather than being eroded by new deployments that bypass the optimization process.
