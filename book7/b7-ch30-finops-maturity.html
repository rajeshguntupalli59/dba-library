Chapter 30: The Database FinOps Maturity Model

Organizations that treat cloud database costs as an afterthought routinely overspend by 40–60% compared to those with a structured optimization practice. That gap is not theoretical. A financial services firm running 200 RDS instances with no tagging strategy, no rightsizing program, and no reserved instance coverage will spend roughly $2.4M annually on compute alone. The same workload, managed through the maturity model described in this chapter, lands closer to $960K — a $1.44M annual difference on compute before touching storage, data transfer, or licensing. This final chapter ties together every technique from the preceding 29 chapters into a single progression framework: the Database FinOps Maturity Model. It gives DBAs a diagnostic checklist to locate where their organization stands today, a structured path to advance through each stage, and the automation scaffolding to sustain those gains indefinitely. The model covers five stages — Reactive, Aware, Structured, Optimized, and Autonomous — and provides the SQL, shell, and Python tooling to measure progress at each one.

---

Stage Definitions: The Five Levels of Database FinOps Maturity

The maturity model borrows its structure from the FinOps Foundation's capability framework but specializes every dimension for database workloads. Each stage has observable characteristics, measurable KPIs, and a ceiling on how much waste it tolerates.

*The five-stage Database FinOps Maturity Model — overspend drops from 50-65% above baseline at Stage 1 to within 5% of theoretical minimum at Stage 5.*

Stage 1 — Reactive

No tagging. No cost allocation by team or workload. Bills are reviewed monthly by finance, not engineering. Storage autoscaling is enabled without upper bounds. Multi-AZ is deployed uniformly across dev, test, and production. Reserved Instance coverage is zero or accidental. The organization is typically 50–65% over its efficient baseline spend.

Stage 2 — Aware

Someone has pulled a Cost Explorer report. There is a spreadsheet. Tagging exists for some resources but not enforced by policy. A few engineers have looked at Performance Insights. There is informal discussion about reserved instances at renewal time. Waste is visible but unowned. Overspend sits at 35–50% above baseline.

Stage 3 — Structured

Tagging is enforced by AWS Config rules, Azure Policy, or GCP Organization Policy. Cost is allocated to teams through showback dashboards. Rightsizing recommendations from Compute Optimizer are reviewed monthly. At least 60% of steady-state compute is covered by Reserved Instances or Committed Use Discounts. Storage tiers are differentiated between environments. Overspend is 15–30% above baseline.

Stage 4 — Optimized

Reserved Instance coverage exceeds 80% for production. Dev and test environments use scheduled start/stop automation. Connection pooling is deployed. Query-level cost attribution exists through Query Store or Performance Insights. Autoscaling policies have tested upper bounds. Storage has been migrated from gp2 to gp3 where appropriate. Overspend is 5–15% above theoretical minimum.

Stage 5 — Autonomous

Cost guardrails are embedded in CI/CD pipelines. Anomaly detection alerts fire before the monthly bill. Rightsizing runs on a weekly automated schedule and tickets are auto-created. Reserved Instance coverage is managed programmatically with expiration tracking. The team has a published cost-per-transaction metric reviewed in sprint retrospectives. Overspend is within 5% of theoretical minimum, with variance explained and documented.

Stage 1 to Stage 2: Building Visibility

You cannot optimize what you cannot see. The transition from Reactive to Aware requires three foundational pieces: mandatory tagging, cost allocation, and a baseline query that tells you where the money actually goes.

The following query runs against the AWS Cost and Usage Report exported to S3 and queried through Athena. It produces the first honest picture of database spend by service and resource type.

-- AWS Athena query against Cost and Usage Report (CUR)
-- Prerequisite: CUR enabled, exported to S3, Athena table created via AWS Glue crawler
-- Run this monthly to establish your baseline

SELECT
    line_item_resource_id                          AS resource_id,
    product_instance_type                          AS instance_type,
    product_database_engine                        AS engine,
    line_item_usage_type                           AS usage_type,
    resource_tags_user_environment                 AS environment,
    resource_tags_user_team                        AS team,
    resource_tags_user_application                 AS application,
    SUM(line_item_unblended_cost)                  AS total_cost_usd,
    SUM(line_item_usage_amount)                    AS total_usage_hours,
    ROUND(
        SUM(line_item_unblended_cost) /
        NULLIF(SUM(line_item_usage_amount), 0), 4
    )                                              AS effective_hourly_rate
FROM
    your_cur_database.your_cur_table
WHERE
    line_item_line_item_type IN ('Usage', 'SavingsPlanCoveredUsage')
    AND product_service_code IN ('AmazonRDS')
    AND line_item_usage_start_date >= DATE_TRUNC('month', CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '1' MONTH)
    AND line_item_usage_start_date <  DATE_TRUNC('month', CURRENT_DATE)
GROUP BY
    1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7
ORDER BY
    total_cost_usd DESC
LIMIT 100;

A result showing resource_tags_user_environment = NULL for more than 20% of resources means tagging compliance is your first remediation target. Every untagged resource is an unowned cost.

On Azure, the equivalent baseline pull uses the Cost Management API:

#!/bin/bash
# Azure CLI: pull RDS-equivalent (Azure SQL, Flexible Server) costs for last 30 days
# Requires: az login, Billing Reader role on subscription

SUBSCRIPTION_ID="your-subscription-id"
START_DATE=$(date -d "30 days ago" +%Y-%m-%d)

  --timeframe "Custom" \
  --time-period start="${START_DATE}" end="${END_DATE}" \

  --dataset-grouping \
    '[{"type":"Dimension","name":"ResourceType"},
      {"type":"Dimension","name":"ResourceGroup"},
      {"type":"TagKey","name":"environment"},
      {"type":"TagKey","name":"team"}]' \

    '{"and":[{"or":[
      {"dimensions":{"name":"ResourceType","operator":"In",

      }]}]}' \
  --output table \
  | tee azure_db_baseline_$(date +%Y%m).txt

echo "Baseline saved to azure_db_baseline_$(date +%Y%m).txt"

On GCP, the equivalent uses BigQuery against the exported billing table:

-- GCP BigQuery: database service cost baseline
-- Prerequisite: Billing export enabled to BigQuery dataset

SELECT
  service.description                                    AS service,
  sku.description                                        AS sku,
  labels.value                                           AS environment,
  project.id                                             AS project,

  SUM(usage.amount)                                      AS total_usage,

  AND _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN

  1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 9

Stage 2 to Stage 3: Building Structure

Structure means cost is owned, not just observed. The three pillars are tagging enforcement, environment tiering, and Reserved Instance planning.

Tagging Enforcement

An AWS Config rule that flags untagged RDS instances fires within 15 minutes of resource creation:

#!/bin/bash
# Deploy AWS Config rule to detect untagged RDS instances
# Required tags: environment, team, application, cost-center
# Remediation: SNS notification + Jira ticket creation (see Python script below)

aws configservice put-config-rule \
  --config-rule '{
    "ConfigRuleName": "rds-required-tags",
    "Description": "Ensures all RDS instances carry required cost allocation tags",
    "Scope": {
      "ComplianceResourceTypes": [
        "AWS::RDS::DBInstance",
        "AWS::RDS::DBCluster"
      ]
    },
    "Source": {
      "Owner": "AWS",
      "SourceIdentifier": "REQUIRED_TAGS"
    },
    "InputParameters": "{
      \"tag1Key\": \"environment\",
      \"tag2Key\": \"team\",
      \"tag3Key\": \"application\",
      \"tag4Key\": \"cost-center\"
    }",
    "ConfigRuleState": "ACTIVE"
  }' \
  --region us-east-1

# Wire SNS notification for non-compliant resources
aws configservice put-remediation-configurations \
  --remediation-configurations '[{
    "ConfigRuleName": "rds-required-tags",
    "TargetType": "SSM_DOCUMENT",
    "TargetId": "AWS-PublishSNSNotification",
    "Parameters": {
      "TopicArn": {
        "StaticValue": {
          "Values": ["arn:aws:sns:us-east-1:123456789:db-finops-alerts"]
        }
      },
      "Message": {
        "StaticValue": {
          "Values": ["RDS resource missing required cost tags. Review immediately."]
        }
      }
    },
    "Automatic": true,
    "MaximumAutomaticAttempts": 3,
    "RetryAttemptSeconds": 60
  }]'

echo "Config rule deployed. Non-compliant resources will trigger SNS within 15 minutes."

Reserved Instance Coverage Analysis

The single highest-return action at Stage 3 is Reserved Instance coverage. The math on a db.r6g.2xlarge in us-east-1 running 24/7:

  • → On-Demand: $0.960/hr × 8,760 hrs = **$8,409.60/year**
  • → 1-year No Upfront RI: $0.576/hr × 8,760 hrs = **$5,045.76/year**
  • → 1-year All Upfront RI: $4,893/year
  • → 3-year All Upfront RI: $2,942/year

    • Moving from On-Demand to a 1-year No Upfront RI saves $3,363.84 per instance per year. A fleet of 50 such instances saves $168,192/year without any architectural change.

    The following Python script analyzes your current RDS fleet, calculates On-Demand vs. RI spend, and generates a prioritized purchase recommendation:

    #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
rds_ri_analyzer.py
Analyzes RDS fleet for Reserved Instance opportunities.
Runs weekly via EventBridge + Lambda or cron.
Outputs: JSON report + SNS notification if savings > threshold.

Author: Database FinOps Toolkit
Requires: boto3, pandas
pip install boto3 pandas
"""

import boto3
import json
import pandas as pd
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
from collections import defaultdict


REGIONS = ["us-east-1", "us-west-2", "eu-west-1"]
SAVINGS_THRESHOLD_USD = 5000          # alert if monthly savings opportunity exceeds this
SNS_TOPIC_ARN = "arn:aws:sns:us-east-1:123456789:db-finops-alerts"

# On-Demand hourly rates for common RDS for SQL Server instance types (us-east-1, License Included)
# Update quarterly from https://aws.amazon.com/rds/pricing/
ON_DEMAND_RATES = {
    "db.t3.micro":     0.017,
    "db.t3.small":     0.034,
    "db.t3.medium":    0.068,
    "db.t3.large":     0.136,
    "db.t3.xlarge":    0.272,
    "db.t3.2xlarge":   0.544,
    "db.m6g.large":    0.171,
    "db.m6g.xlarge":   0.342,
    "db.m6g.2xlarge":  0.684,
    "db.m6g.4xlarge":  1.368,
    "db.m6g.8xlarge":  2.736,
    "db.r6g.large":    0.240,
    "db.r6g.xlarge":   0.480,
    "db.r6g.2xlarge":  0.960,
    "db.r6g.4xlarge":  1.920,
    "db.r6g.8xlarge":  3.840,
    "db.r6g.16xlarge": 7.680,
}

# 1-year No Upfront RI effective hourly rates (us-east-1)
RI_1YR_NO_UPFRONT_RATES = {
    "db.t3.micro":     0.011,
    "db.t3.small":     0.022,
    "db.t3.medium":    0.044,
    "db.t3.large":     0.087,
    "db.t3.xlarge":    0.174,
    "db.t3.2xlarge":   0.347,
    "db.m6g.large":    0.103,
    "db.m6g.xlarge":   0.205,
    "db.m6g.2xlarge":  0.410,
    "db.m6g.4xlarge":  0.821,
    "db.m6g.8xlarge":  1.641,
    "db.r6g.large":    0.144,
    "db.r6g.xlarge":   0.288,
    "db.r6g.2xlarge":  0.576,
    "db.r6g.4xlarge":  1.152,
    "db.r6g.8xlarge":  2.304,
    "db.r6g.16xlarge": 4.608,
}

HOURS_PER_MONTH = 730


def get_rds_instances(region: str) -> list[dict]:
    """Return all RDS instances in a region with key attributes."""
    client = boto3.client("rds", region_name=region)
    paginator = client.get_paginator("describe_db_instances")
    instances = []
    for page in paginator.paginate():
        for db in page["DBInstances"]:
            instances.append({
                "identifier":    db["DBInstanceIdentifier"],
                "instance_class": db["DBInstanceClass"],
                "engine":        db["Engine"],
                "status":        db["DBInstanceStatus"],
                "multi_az":      db.get("MultiAZ", False),
                "region":        region,
                "environment":   next(
                    (t["Value"] for t in db.get("TagList", [])
                     if t["Key"] == "environment"), "untagged"
                ),
                "team":          next(
                    (t["Value"] for t in db.get("TagList", [])
                     if t["Key"] == "team"), "untagged"
                ),
            })
    return instances


def get_existing_ri_coverage(region: str) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Return count of active RIs per instance class in a region."""
    client = boto3.client("rds", region_name=region)
    response = client.describe_reserved_db_instances()
    coverage = defaultdict(int)
    for ri in response["ReservedDBInstances"]:
        if ri["State"] == "active":
            coverage[ri["DBInstanceClass"]] += ri["DBInstanceCount"]
    return coverage


def analyze_fleet(regions: list[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Build a DataFrame of all instances with cost and RI gap analysis."""
    rows = []
    for region in regions:
        instances   = get_rds_instances(region)
        ri_coverage = get_existing_ri_coverage(region)

        # Count On-Demand instances per class (exclude non-running)
        class_counts: dict[str, int] = defaultdict(int)
        for inst in instances:
            if inst["status"] == "available":
                class_counts[inst["instance_class"]] += 1

        for inst_class, count in class_counts.items():
            od_rate  = ON_DEMAND_RATES.get(inst_class, 0.0)
            ri_rate  = RI_1YR_NO_UPFRONT_RATES.get(inst_class, 0.0)
            covered  = ri_coverage.get(inst_class, 0)
            exposed  = max(0, count - covered)
            saving   = (od_rate - ri_rate) * HOURS_PER_MONTH * exposed

            rows.append({
                "region":             region,
                "instance_class":     inst_class,
                "running_count":      count,
                "ri_covered":         covered,
                "on_demand_exposed":  exposed,
                "od_rate_hr":         od_rate,
                "ri_rate_hr":         ri_rate,
                "discount_pct":       round((1 - ri_rate / od_rate) * 100, 1) if od_rate else 0,
                "monthly_od_cost":    round(od_rate  * HOURS_PER_MONTH * exposed, 2),
                "monthly_ri_cost":    round(ri_rate  * HOURS_PER_MONTH * exposed, 2),
                "monthly_savings":    round(saving, 2),
                "annual_savings":     round(saving * 12, 2),
            })

    df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    return df.sort_values("monthly_savings", ascending=False)


def publish_report(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    """Publish top opportunities to SNS if threshold exceeded."""
    total_monthly  = df["monthly_savings"].sum()
    total_annual   = df["annual_savings"].sum()

    report = {
        "generated_at":      datetime.utcnow().isoformat(),
        "total_monthly_opp": round(total_monthly, 2),
        "total_annual_opp":  round(total_annual, 2),
        "top_10_actions":    df.head(10).to_dict(orient="records"),
    }

    print(json.dumps(report, indent=2))

    if total_monthly >= SAVINGS_THRESHOLD_USD:
        sns = boto3.client("sns", region_name="us-east-1")
        sns.publish(
            TopicArn=SNS_TOPIC_ARN,
            Subject=f"[DB FinOps] RI Opportunity: ${total_monthly:,.0f}/mo (${total_annual:,.0f}/yr)",
            Message=json.dumps(report, indent=2),
        )
        print(f"Alert published to SNS. Total monthly opportunity: ${total_monthly:,.2f}")


def main():
    df = analyze_fleet(REGIONS)
    publish_report(df)
    df.to_csv(f"ri_analysis_{datetime.utcnow().strftime('%Y%m%d')}.csv", index=False)
    print("Analysis complete. CSV saved.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

    Run this on a weekly schedule. Over a 50-instance fleet, a single run that surfaces a $168K annual opportunity pays for every automation investment the team will ever make.

    Stage 3 to Stage 4: Operational Optimization

    Stage 4 is where the cost savings become granular, persistent, and embedded in daily operations. Four practices define this transition: query-level cost attribution, storage tier optimization, environment scheduling, and connection pooling.

    Query-Level Cost Attribution

    The cheapest query is the one that never runs. Query Store on SQL Server surfaces which queries drive the most I/O, CPU, and buffer-pool pressure — all of which translate directly to instance size requirements.

    On SQL Server (Azure SQL / RDS for SQL Server), the equivalent uses sys.dm_exec_query_stats:

    -- SQL Server: top resource-consuming queries with execution cost attribution
-- Run in Azure SQL or RDS for SQL Server
-- Results guide rightsizing: if top queries are I/O-bound, storage throughput is the bottleneck

SELECT TOP 25
    qs.execution_count,
    qs.total_logical_reads / qs.execution_count          AS avg_logical_reads,
    qs.total_physical_reads / qs.execution_count         AS avg_physical_reads,
    qs.total_worker_time / qs.execution_count / 1000     AS avg_cpu_ms,
    qs.total_elapsed_time / qs.execution_count / 1000    AS avg_elapsed_ms,
    qs.total_grant_kb / NULLIF(qs.execution_count, 0)    AS avg_memory_grant_kb,
    qs.total_spills / NULLIF(qs.execution_count, 0)      AS avg_spills,
    -- Cost score: weighted sum of CPU + I/O + memory pressure
    ROUND(
        (qs.total_worker_time    * 0.40 +
         qs.total_logical_reads  * 0.35 +
         qs.total_grant_kb       * 0.25) / 1000000.0
    , 2)                                                 AS weighted_cost_score,
    DB_NAME(qt.dbid)                                     AS database_name,
    OBJECT_NAME(qt.objectid, qt.dbid)                    AS object_name,
    SUBSTRING(
        qt.text,
        qs.statement_start_offset / 2,
        (CASE WHEN qs.statement_end_offset = -1
              THEN LEN(CONVERT(NVARCHAR(MAX), qt.text)) * 2
              ELSE qs.statement_end_offset
         END - qs.statement_start_offset) / 2
    )                                                    AS query_text
FROM
    sys.dm_exec_query_stats qs
    CROSS APPLY sys.dm_exec_sql_text(qs.sql_handle) qt
WHERE
    qs.execution_count > 50
ORDER BY
    weighted_cost_score DESC;

    Storage Tier Migration

    The gp2-to-gp3 migration math at scale is unavoidable. A 1TB gp2 volume on RDS costs $115/month. The same capacity on gp3 costs $92/month — a saving of $23/month or $276/year per volume. A fleet of 100 production databases averaging 1TB each saves $27,600/year from one storage change with no performance regression. For volumes above 1TB, gp3 has an additional advantage: gp2 delivers 3 IOPS/GB (baseline), meaning a 1TB gp2 volume gets 3,000 IOPS baseline and you pay for more by provisioning io1. gp3 provides 3,000 IOPS baseline at no extra cost and allows independent IOPS scaling up to 16,000 IOPS without tying throughput to storage size.

echo "Scanning for gp2 volumes in ${REGION}..."

aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --region "${REGION}" \
  --query "DBInstances[*].{
    ID:DBInstanceIdentifier,
    Class:DBInstanceClass,
    StorageType:StorageType,
    AllocatedStorage:AllocatedStorage,
    Status:DBInstanceStatus,
    MultiAZ:MultiAZ}" \
  --output json | \

    Environment Scheduling

    Non-production databases running 24/7 are one of the most common and correctable sources of waste. A db.m6g.xlarge in us-east-1 costs $0.342/hr On-Demand. Running it only during business hours — 10 hours/weekday, 50 weeks/year — reduces runtime from 8,760 hours to 2,500 hours, cutting annual compute cost from $2,994 to $855, a saving of $2,139 per instance. Across 30 development and staging databases, that is $64,170/year from scheduled stop/start alone.

    #!/bin/bash
# EventBridge-triggered Lambda alternative: direct CLI scheduling via cron on EC2 bastion
# For Lambda-based approach, wrap these commands in a Python Lambda function
# triggered by two EventBridge rules: start (07:00 Mon-Fri) and stop (19:00 Mon-Fri)

# Tag dev/staging instances with schedule=business-hours before running

REGION="us-east-1"
ACTION="${1:-stop}"   # pass "start" or "stop"

if [[ "${ACTION}" != "start" && "${ACTION}" != "stop" ]]; then
    echo "Usage: $0 [start|stop]"
    exit 1
fi

echo "Action: ${ACTION} | Region: ${REGION} | Time: $(date)"

# Find all instances tagged schedule=business-hours
INSTANCE_IDS=$(aws rds describe-db-instances \
  --region "${REGION}" \
  --query "DBInstances[*].DBInstanceIdentifier" \
  --output text)

for INSTANCE_ID in ${INSTANCE_IDS}; do
    SCHEDULE_TAG=$(aws rds list-tags-for-resource \
      --resource-name "arn:aws:rds:${REGION}:$(aws sts get-caller-identity --query Account --output text):db:${INSTANCE_ID}" \
      --query "TagList[?Key=='schedule'].Value" \
      --output text \
      --region "${REGION}")

    if [[ "${SCHEDULE_TAG}" == "business-hours" ]]; then
        STATUS=$(aws rds describe-db-instances \
          --db-instance-identifier "${INSTANCE_ID}" \
          --query "DBInstances[0].DBInstanceStatus" \
          --output text \
          --region "${REGION}")

        if [[ "${ACTION}" == "stop" && "${STATUS}" == "available" ]]; then
            echo "Stopping: ${INSTANCE_ID}"
            aws rds stop-db-instance \
              --db-instance-identifier "${INSTANCE_ID}" \
              --region "${REGION}" \
              --output text
        elif [[ "${ACTION}" == "start" && "${STATUS}" == "stopped" ]]; then
            echo "Starting: ${INSTANCE_ID}"
            aws rds start-db-instance \
              --db-instance-identifier "${INSTANCE_ID}" \
              --region "${REGION}" \
              --output text
        else
            echo "Skipping: ${INSTANCE_ID} (status: ${STATUS})"
        fi
    fi
done

echo "Schedule action '${ACTION}' complete at $(date)"

    Deploy two EventBridge rules pointing at a Lambda that calls this logic: one firing at 07:00 UTC Monday–Friday to start, one at 19:00 UTC Monday–Friday to stop. Add a third rule at 08:00 Saturday to catch any instances that did not stop Friday evening.

    Stage 4 to Stage 5: Autonomous Cost Governance

    Stage 5 is not a destination teams reach once — it is a practice they sustain continuously. The defining characteristic is that cost drift is detected and corrected automatically, without requiring a human to notice a bill. Three systems make this work: anomaly detection with automatic remediation, RI lifecycle management, and cost-per-transaction instrumentation embedded in application telemetry.

    Anomaly Detection and Automatic Remediation

    AWS Cost Anomaly Detection can be configured via CLI and will fire SNS alerts when spend deviates from a learned baseline. But the more precise control comes from building your own detector against CloudWatch metrics, because CloudWatch catches the infrastructure event that caused the cost spike before the billing system does.

    The following Python script runs every 15 minutes via EventBridge Scheduler. It checks five leading indicators of cost overrun: CPU utilization trending above the instance's efficient operating band, FreeStorageSpace falling below the autoscale-trigger threshold, DatabaseConnections exceeding the connection limit (signaling a missing connection pool), ReadIOPS and WriteIOPS approaching the provisioned limit (signaling a pending io1 charge), and ReadLatency spiking above 20ms (signaling a storage bottleneck that will drive a support ticket to upsize storage).

Monitors RDS CloudWatch metrics for leading indicators of cost overrun.

opens a Jira ticket via REST API.

  JIRA_URL             - optional, e.g. https://yourorg.atlassian.net
  JIRA_API_TOKEN       - optional
  JIRA_PROJECT_KEY     - optional, e.g. DBOPS

DYNAMO_TABLE    = os.environ.get("DYNAMODB_TABLE", "db-finops-anomalies")
JIRA_URL        = os.environ.get("JIRA_URL", "")
JIRA_TOKEN      = os.environ.get("JIRA_API_TOKEN", "")

THRESHOLDS = {
    "CPUUtilization":       {"warning": 70.0,  "critical": 85.0,  "unit": "%"},

    "ReadIOPS":             {"warning": 0.80,  "critical": 0.90,  "unit": "ratio","dynamic": True},

@dataclass
class Anomaly:
    instance_id:    str
    instance_class: str
    metric:         str
    current_value:  float
    threshold:      float
    severity:       str
    unit:           str
    recommendation: str
    timestamp:      str

def get_rds_instances() -> list[dict]:
    rds = boto3.client("rds", region_name=REGION)
    paginator = rds.get_paginator("describe_db_instances")
    instances = []
    for page in paginator.paginate():
        for db in page["DBInstances"]:
            if db["DBInstanceStatus"] == "available":
                instances.append({
                    "id":    db["DBInstanceIdentifier"],
                    "class": db["DBInstanceClass"],
                    "storage_gb": db["AllocatedStorage"],
                    "iops":  db.get("Iops", 3000),
                })
    return instances


def get_metric_average(cw, instance_id: str, metric: str,
                       minutes: int = 15) -> Optional[float]:
    end   = datetime.now(timezone.utc)
    start = end - timedelta(minutes=minutes)
    resp  = cw.get_metric_statistics(
        Namespace="AWS/RDS",
        MetricName=metric,
        Dimensions=[{"Name": "DBInstanceIdentifier", "Value": instance_id}],
        StartTime=start,
        EndTime=end,
        Period=minutes * 60,
        Statistics=["Average"],
    )
    if not resp["Datapoints"]:
        return None
    return resp["Datapoints"][0]["Average"]


def evaluate_instance(cw, instance: dict) -> list[Anomaly]:
    anomalies = []
    inst_id    = instance["id"]
    inst_class = instance["class"]
    max_conns  = MAX_CONNECTIONS_BY_CLASS.get(inst_class, 1000)
    prov_iops  = instance["iops"]

    checks = [
        ("CPUUtilization",      None,       None),
        ("FreeStorageSpace",    None,       None),
        ("DatabaseConnections", max_conns,  None),
        ("ReadIOPS",            prov_iops,  None),
        ("WriteIOPS",           prov_iops,  None),
        ("ReadLatency",         None,       None),
        ("WriteLatency",        None,       None),
    ]

    for metric, cap, _ in checks:
        value = get_metric_average(cw, inst_id, metric)
        if value is None:
            continue

        cfg = THRESHOLDS[metric]

        # Normalize dynamic ratio metrics
        if cfg.get("dynamic") and cap:
            normalized = value / cap
        elif metric == "FreeStorageSpace":
            # Convert bytes to GB for comparison
            normalized = value / (1024 ** 3)
        else:
            normalized = value

        warn_thresh = cfg["warning"]
        crit_thresh = cfg["critical"]
        invert      = cfg.get("invert", False)

        is_warning  = (normalized < warn_thresh) if invert else (normalized >= warn_thresh)
        is_critical = (normalized < crit_thresh) if invert else (normalized >= crit_thresh)

        if not is_warning:
            continue

        severity = "CRITICAL" if is_critical else "WARNING"

        recommendations = {
            "CPUUtilization": (
                f"Instance {inst_class} CPU at {normalized:.1f}%. "
                f"Review Query Store for rogue queries before upsizing. "
                f"Upsizing to next class adds ~40% to compute cost."
            ),
            "FreeStorageSpace": (
                f"Only {normalized:.1f}GB free. Autoscale will trigger, increasing "
                f"storage cost. Archive or purge old data first. "
                f"Each 100GB gp3 expansion adds $9.20/month."
            ),
            "DatabaseConnections": (
                f"Connections at {normalized*100:.0f}% of limit ({int(value)}/{max_conns}). "
                f"Fix client-side connection pooling (Max Pool Size, pooling=true). Typical saving: 60-80% connection "
                f"reduction, enabling downsizing by one instance class."
            ),
            "ReadIOPS": (
                f"Read IOPS at {normalized*100:.0f}% of provisioned ({int(value)}/{prov_iops}). "
                f"Add read replica before provisioning additional IOPS. "
                f"Provisioning 1000 additional IOPS on io1 costs $65/month."
            ),
            "WriteIOPS": (
                f"Write IOPS at {normalized*100:.0f}% of provisioned ({int(value)}/{prov_iops}). "
                f"Check for missing indexes causing full-table writes. "
                f"Provisioning 1000 additional IOPS on io1 costs $65/month."
            ),
            "ReadLatency": (
                f"Read latency {value*1000:.1f}ms exceeds {crit_thresh*1000:.0f}ms threshold. "
                f"Check buffer cache hit ratio and consider readable secondaries "
                f"before migrating to io2 storage."
            ),
            "WriteLatency": (
                f"Write latency {value*1000:.1f}ms exceeds threshold. "
                f"Check for lock contention via sys.dm_tran_locks. "
                f"Premature io1 provisioning adds $0.065 per IOPS-month."
            ),
        }

        risk_map = {
            "CPUUtilization":      45.0 * 12,
            "FreeStorageSpace":    9.20 * 12,
            "DatabaseConnections": 120.0,
            "ReadIOPS":            65.0 * 12,
            "WriteIOPS":           65.0 * 12,
            "ReadLatency":         75.0 * 12,
            "WriteLatency":        75.0 * 12,
        }

        anomalies.append(Anomaly(
            instance_id=inst_id,
            instance_class=inst_class,
            metric=metric,
            current_value=round(normalized, 4),
            threshold=warn_thresh,
            severity=severity,
            unit=cfg["unit"],
            recommendation=recommendations[metric],
            timestamp=datetime.utcnow().isoformat(),
            estimated_monthly_risk_usd=round(risk_map.get(metric, 0) / 12, 2),
        ))

    return anomalies


def publish_anomalies(anomalies: list[Anomaly]) -> None:
    if not anomalies:
        return

    sns     = boto3.client("sns",      region_name=REGION)
    dynamo  = boto3.resource("dynamodb", region_name=REGION)
    table   = dynamo.Table(DYNAMO_TABLE)

    total_risk = sum(a.estimated_monthly_risk_usd for a in anomalies)
    critical   = [a for a in anomalies if a.severity == "CRITICAL"]

    summary = (
        f"DB FinOps Anomaly Report | {datetime.utcnow().strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M')} UTC\n"
        f"Total anomalies: {len(anomalies)} "
        f"({len(critical)} critical)\n"
        f"Estimated monthly cost risk: ${total_risk:,.2f}\n\n"
    )
    for a in anomalies:
        summary += (
            f"[{a.severity}] {a.instance_id} | {a.metric}: {a.current_value} {a.unit}\n"
            f"  -> {a.recommendation}\n\n"
        )

    sns.publish(
        TopicArn=SNS_TOPIC_ARN,
        Subject=f"[DB FinOps] {len(critical)} Critical Anomalies | ${total_risk:,.0f}/mo risk",
        Message=summary,
    )

    for a in anomalies:
        table.put_item(Item={
            **asdict(a),
            "ttl": int(time.time()) + (90 * 86400),  # 90-day retention
        })

    if JIRA_URL and JIRA_TOKEN and critical:
        open_jira_tickets(critical)


def open_jira_tickets(anomalies: list[Anomaly]) -> None:
    credentials = base64.b64encode(f"automation@yourorg.com:{JIRA_TOKEN}".encode()).decode()
    headers = {
        "Authorization": f"Basic {credentials}",
        "Content-Type":  "application/json",
    }
    for a in anomalies:
        payload = json.dumps({
            "fields": {
                "project":     {"key": JIRA_PROJECT},
                "summary":     f"[DB FinOps] CRITICAL: {a.instance_id} | {a.metric}",
                "description": {
                    "type":    "doc",
                    "version": 1,
                    "content": [{
                        "type":    "paragraph",
                        "content": [{"type": "text", "text": a.recommendation}]
                    }]
                },
                "issuetype":   {"name": "Task"},
                "priority":    {"name": "High"},
                "labels":      ["db-finops", "cost-anomaly", a.instance_id],
            }
        }).encode()

        req = urllib.request.Request(
            f"{JIRA_URL}/rest/api/3/issue",
            data=payload,
            headers=headers,
            method="POST",
        )
        try:
            with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=10) as resp:
                result = json.loads(resp.read())
                print(f"Jira ticket created: {result.get('key')} for {a.instance_id}")
        except Exception as exc:
            print(f"Jira ticket creation failed for {a.instance_id}: {exc}")


def lambda_handler(event, context):
    """Entry point for AWS Lambda. Also callable directly."""
    cw        = boto3.client("cloudwatch", region_name=REGION)
    instances = get_rds_instances()
    print(f"Evaluating {len(instances)} instances across {REGION}")

    all_anomalies: list[Anomaly] = []
    for inst in instances:
        found = evaluate_instance(cw, inst)
        all_anomalies.extend(found)

    print(f"Found {len(all_anomalies)} anomalies")
    publish_anomalies(all_anomalies)
    return {"anomalies": len(all_anomalies)}


if __name__ == "__main__":
    lambda_handler({}, None)

    Cost-Per-Transaction Instrumentation

    Stage 5 teams measure cost at the transaction level, not the resource level. This requires correlating billing data with application throughput metrics. The simplest implementation uses a CloudWatch custom metric published by the application alongside a monthly cost allocation script.

    #!/bin/bash
# Publish cost-per-transaction metric to CloudWatch
# Run at end of each UTC day via cron: 0 0 * * * /opt/finops/cost_per_txn.sh
#
# Prerequisites:
#   - CloudWatch agent publishing db_transactions_total metric from app
#   - AWS Cost Explorer API access
#   - jq installed

REGION="us-east-1"
DB_IDENTIFIER="prod-sqlserver-primary"
NAMESPACE="DatabaseFinOps"
DATE_YESTERDAY=$(date -d "yesterday" +%Y-%m-%d)
DATE_TODAY=$(date +%Y-%m-%d)

echo "Computing cost-per-transaction for ${DATE_YESTERDAY}"

# Get yesterday's transaction count from CloudWatch (published by app)
TXN_COUNT=$(aws cloudwatch get-metric-statistics \
  --region "${REGION}" \
  --namespace "Application/Database" \
  --metric-name "db_transactions_total" \
  --dimensions Name=DBIdentifier,Value="${DB_IDENTIFIER}" \
  --start-time "${DATE_YESTERDAY}T00:00:00Z" \
  --end-time   "${DATE_TODAY}T00:00:00Z" \
  --period     86400 \
  --statistics Sum \
  --query "Datapoints[0].Sum" \
  --output text)

# Get yesterday's actual RDS cost from Cost Explorer
DAILY_COST=$(aws ce get-cost-and-usage \
  --time-period Start="${DATE_YESTERDAY}",End="${DATE_TODAY}" \
  --granularity DAILY \
  --filter '{
    "And": [
      {"Dimensions": {"Key":"SERVICE","Values":["Amazon RDS"]}},
      {"Tags": {"Key":"application","Values":["prod-sqlserver"]}}
    ]
  }' \
  --metrics "UnblendedCost" \
  --query "ResultsByTime[0].Total.UnblendedCost.Amount" \
  --output text \
  --region "${REGION}")

if [[ "${TXN_COUNT}" == "None" || "${TXN_COUNT}" == "0" ]]; then
    echo "No transaction data available for ${DATE_YESTERDAY}. Skipping."
    exit 0
fi

COST_PER_TXN=$(echo "scale=8; ${DAILY_COST} / ${TXN_COUNT}" | bc)

echo "Date: ${DATE_YESTERDAY}"
echo "Transactions: ${TXN_COUNT}"
echo "DB Cost USD:  ${DAILY_COST}"
echo "Cost/Txn USD: ${COST_PER_TXN}"

# Publish to CloudWatch as custom metric
aws cloudwatch put-metric-data \
  --region "${REGION}" \
  --namespace "${NAMESPACE}" \
  --metric-data "[
    {
      \"MetricName\": \"CostPerTransaction\",
      \"Dimensions\": [{\"Name\":\"DBIdentifier\",\"Value\":\"${DB_IDENTIFIER}\"}],
      \"Timestamp\": \"${DATE_YESTERDAY}T12:00:00Z\",
      \"Value\": ${COST_PER_TXN},
      \"Unit\": \"None\"
    },
    {
      \"MetricName\": \"DailyDBCostUSD\",
      \"Dimensions\": [{\"Name\":\"DBIdentifier\",\"Value\":\"${DB_IDENTIFIER}\"}],
      \"Timestamp\": \"${DATE_YESTERDAY}T12:00:00Z\",
      \"Value\": ${DAILY_COST},
      \"Unit\": \"None\"
    }
  ]"

echo "Metrics published to CloudWatch namespace ${NAMESPACE}"

    When CostPerTransaction trends upward while transaction volume holds flat, the signal is unambiguous: something changed in the infrastructure or query patterns that is inflating cost without delivering more throughput. That is the leading indicator that triggers investigation before the monthly bill arrives.

    Measuring Maturity: The Database FinOps Scorecard

    A maturity model without measurement is a philosophy. The scorecard below assigns points across ten dimensions, producing a score from 0 to 100. Score below 40: Stage 1 or 2. Score 40–65: Stage 3. Score 65–85: Stage 4. Score above 85: Stage 5.

    | Dimension | Max Points | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Stage 4 | Stage 5 |

    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

    | Tag compliance rate | 10 | 0–2 | 3–4 | 5–7 | 8–9 | 10 |

    | RI/CUD coverage (prod) | 15 | 0 | 1–4 | 5–9 | 10–13 | 14–15 |

    | Env scheduling (non-prod) | 10 | 0 | 0–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–10 |

    | Storage tier optimization | 10 | 0 | 0–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–10 |

    | Connection pooling deployed | 5 | 0 | 0 | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5 |

    | Query cost attribution | 10 | 0 | 1–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–10 |

    | Rightsizing program active | 15 | 0 | 1–4 | 5–9 | 10–13 | 14–15 |

    | Anomaly detection automated | 10 | 0 | 0–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–10 |

    | Cost-per-transaction tracked | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0–3 | 4–7 | 8–10 |

    | CI/CD cost gates active | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0–1 | 2–3 | 4–5 |

    Run this scorecard quarterly. Record it in your team wiki. A team that moves from 35 to 65 over 12 months will typically realize 25–35% reduction in database spend during the same period, because the scorecard dimensions are not vanity metrics — each one maps directly to a budget line.

    The complete annual savings potential by stage transition, on a fleet of 100 production RDS instances averaging db.r6g.2xlarge with 1TB gp2 storage, Multi-AZ:

  • → **Stage 1 → Stage 2**: Tagging and visibility — $0 direct saving, but identifies $400K+ in misallocated spend
  • → **Stage 2 → Stage 3**: RI coverage at 80% + gp2→gp3 migration — **$280,000/year**
  • → **Stage 3 → Stage 4**: Rightsizing + env scheduling + connection pooling — **$195,000/year**
  • → **Stage 4 → Stage 5**: Anomaly prevention + query optimization + cost-per-txn tracking — **$85,000/year**
  • → **Total cumulative saving: $560,000/year on a $1.4M baseline — a 40% reduction**

    • These figures use 2024 us-east-1 pricing for AWS RDS. Azure and GCP equivalents land within 8–12% of these numbers given similar fleet composition.

  • → **Maturity is measurable.** The five-stage model — Reactive, Aware, Structured, Optimized, Autonomous — provides a scoring system that converts abstract "cost culture" into a number your team can track quarterly.
